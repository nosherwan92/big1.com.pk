Incorporating is not the hard part. Most people are surprised by how much arrives *after* the certificate.

## Step one: reserve the name

You propose a name to SECP and it is checked against existing companies and registered trademarks. Names that are deceptively similar to something already on the register, or that imply a connection you do not have, are refused.

Have a second and third choice ready. And check the name against trademarks before you fall in love with it, a company name and a trademark are different registers, and owning one does not give you the other.

## Step two: decide the structure

- **Single-member company (SMC)**, one shareholder, limited liability
- **Private limited**, the common choice, two or more shareholders
- **Public limited**, for wider ownership, with heavier requirements

The choice affects who can own shares, how many directors you need, and how much you will file each year. It can be converted later, but conversion is more work than choosing correctly now.

## Step three: incorporate

Documents are filed with SECP: the memorandum and articles, the directors' and subscribers' details, the registered office address. On approval, the certificate of incorporation is issued.

## Step four: everything after

This is the part that catches people out. A new company typically needs:

- Its **own NTN**, separate from the directors'
- A **bank account** in the company's name
- **Sales tax registration**, if it will be making taxable supplies
- **Annual filings** with SECP, and returns with FBR, from the first year onward
- Records of any change (directors, address, shareholding) reported within the prescribed time

A company that is incorporated and then left alone still accrues obligations. Dormancy is not the same as absence.

## The practical advice

Decide the structure and the name first, and check the name against both registers. Budget for the year-one filings, not only the incorporation. And keep the statutory records from day one, because reconstructing them later is far more expensive than maintaining them.
